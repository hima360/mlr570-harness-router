\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study shows that the harness is not a neutral container
for an LLM, but an important design variable in agentic
systems. Across 480 runs involving three models and four
harnesses, changing the harness alone moved the pass rate
of GLM-5.3 Flash from 10\% to 40\%, while GPT-6.1 Sol
achieved similar pass rates at a sevenfold difference in
cost. In Claude Code, all three models solved 16 of 40
runs, with GLM-5.3 Flash achieving the lowest cost per
solve at \$0.63. These findings show that a well-matched
harness can allow an inexpensive model to compete with
more expensive alternatives.

Our proposed cascade harness (v2.8) keeps tasks whole
and postpones the routing decision until the inexpensive
model has attempted the task. On 18 evaluation tasks,
the cascade solved 11 of 36 runs (31\%), compared with
4 of 35 (11\%) for Claude Opus 5.5 alone. It reduced
cost per solved task by 51\%, from \$7.83 to \$3.80,
although total cost increased by 34\%. While the
improvement is not statistically significant at this
sample size ($p = 0.079$), the results suggest that an
inexpensive model can contribute useful intermediate work
even when it cannot solve the task independently.

The exploratory provider comparison further suggests
that the serving setup may influence agent performance.
Future work should conduct a controlled provider study
with the model, harness, tasks, reasoning effort and
execution budget held fixed, using larger samples and
repeated runs to isolate provider effects.

Overall, agentic LLM performance and cost efficiency
should be evaluated at the system level rather than
by model name alone. Joint consideration of the model,
harness and serving provider offers a promising direction
for building more cost-efficient agentic systems.
