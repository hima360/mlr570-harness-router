\begin{abstract}
The performance of an agentic large language model (LLM)
depends not only on the model itself but also on the harness
through which it interacts with its environment. We argue
that the harness is not a neutral container, but a design
variable that influences task success, cost and execution
time. We investigate three research questions concerning
model-harness compatibility, cost-efficient model routing
and serving-provider sensitivity.
First, we evaluate three models across four harnesses on
Terminal-Bench 4.0 and SWE-Bench Pro, comprising 480 runs.
GLM-5.3 Flash solves between 4 and 16 of 40 runs depending
on the harness. In Claude Code, it matches Claude Sonnet
5.5 at 16 of 40 solved runs, while costing \$0.63 per
solve compared with \$2.13 at public list prices. Second, we find that
decomposing tightly coupled agentic tasks introduces
coordination failures, motivating whole-task routing.
Third, we develop a cheap-first cascade harness (v2.8)
in which GLM-5.3 Flash attempts the task before Claude
Opus 5.5 reviews the result and takes over when needed.
The cascade solves 11 of 36 evaluation runs, compared
with 4 of 35 for Opus alone, reducing cost per solve
from \$7.83 to \$3.80 (51\%), despite a 34\% increase
in total cost. This improvement is not statistically
significant at the available sample size ($p = 0.079$).
Fourth, an exploratory comparison suggests that serving
providers may influence agent performance, motivating
a controlled provider study.

All costs are calculated at public list prices.
Our findings support evaluating agentic LLM systems
as a combination of model, harness and serving setup,
rather than by model name alone.
\end{abstract}
