\section{Limitations}

\paragraph{Compute budget.}
All experiments ran on consumer subscription plans (Anthropic, OpenAI, Z.ai and Factory) rather than metered API
access, which would have cost substantially more at the scale of this study. Subscription plans cap usage per
5-hour and weekly window, which bounded the number of runs per configuration (40), the number of repeated runs
used to estimate variance (3 tasks $\times$ 3 runs) and the set of models and harnesses we could include. Larger
samples, more repeats per task and additional models and harnesses would narrow the uncertainty in our
comparisons.

\paragraph{Small samples.}
Each Phase~1 configuration has 40 runs, and the Phase~2 evaluation has 18 tasks with two runs per condition on
the first provider and one on the second. Single runs are not stable: in Phase~1, the outcome flipped between
pass and fail in 12 of 36 groups of repeated runs. We therefore treat gaps of one or two tasks as within
run-to-run variance and draw conclusions only from larger differences.

\paragraph{Evaluation tasks.}
The 18 evaluation tasks were part of an early baseline sweep that informed the cascade design, so they are not
fully held out, although no setting was tuned on them after the design was fixed (Section~\ref{sec:p2setup}).
The second evaluation round, with GLM-5.3 Flash served by Z.ai, covered v2.8 and Opus~5.5 alone; GLM-5.3 Flash
alone was not rerun with that provider.

\paragraph{Commercial backends.}
Every model in this study is a commercial service that can change or end. Factory's Droid
subscription, which served both models in the first Phase~2 evaluation round, ended during the evaluation, and
the second round had to use a different provider. We pinned harness versions in Phase~1, but results for newer
harness and model versions may differ.

\paragraph{Cost.}
All costs are tokens times public list prices, not what we paid under our subscriptions. They show the relative
cost of configurations under metered pricing; actual costs depend on the subscription plan.

\paragraph{Router baselines.}
The four routers in our feasibility study (Arch-Router, Laya, the NVIDIA classifier and RouteLLM) were compared
offline only, on 24 author-labelled tasks. Inside the harness only Laya routed steps, the Arch-Router fallback
never fired in logged runs, and we did not build a fine-tuned Laya.

\paragraph{Run conditions.}
Each Phase~1 model was run by a different author on their own machine: two Macs and one Windows machine. The
number of runs executed at the same time also differed: five for Sonnet~5.5 and two for GLM-5.3 Flash and
GPT-6.1 Sol. Wall time depends on the machine and on how many runs share it, so the median times we report are
comparable across harnesses for the same model, but differences in time between models may partly reflect these
run conditions.
